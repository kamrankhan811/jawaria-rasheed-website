PCOS Care

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal condition that affects how the ovaries work. It can cause irregular periods, acne, extra hair growth, weight gain and difficulty getting pregnant. With the right plan, symptoms can be well controlled.

About PCOS Care

PCOS affects roughly one in ten women of reproductive age, and many are not diagnosed for years. Despite the name, PCOS is not about dangerous cysts — the “cysts” are small follicles that have not matured and released an egg.

PCOS is linked with insulin resistance, which explains why it is connected to weight gain and a higher long-term risk of type 2 diabetes. That is why good PCOS care looks beyond periods to your overall health. Dr. Jawaria Rasheed helps women in Bahawalpur manage PCOS according to their own goals — regular cycles, clearer skin, weight management or pregnancy.

Common signs of PCOS

  • ✓Periods that are irregular, infrequent or absent
  • ✓Acne or oily skin
  • ✓Unwanted hair on the face, chest or abdomen
  • ✓Thinning hair on the scalp
  • ✓Weight gain, especially around the waist, or difficulty losing weight
  • ✓Darkened skin patches on the neck or underarms
  • ✓Difficulty getting pregnant

How PCOS is diagnosed

  • Symptoms and history, especially your period pattern.
  • Blood tests for hormones such as testosterone, and to rule out thyroid or prolactin problems.
  • Pelvic ultrasound to look at the ovaries.
  • Metabolic checks — blood sugar, cholesterol and blood pressure.

How PCOS is managed

Lifestyle changes

Balanced eating and regular activity. Even 5–10% weight loss, when appropriate, can restore regular cycles.

Regulating periods

Hormonal treatment to protect the lining of the uterus and regulate bleeding.

Skin and hair

Treatment for acne and unwanted hair growth.

Insulin resistance

Medicines such as metformin in selected women.

Fertility support

Ovulation induction, follicle monitoring and IUI for women trying to conceive.

Living well with PCOS

PCOS is a long-term condition, but it is very manageable. Regular follow-up helps adjust treatment as your goals change — for example, from regulating periods to planning a pregnancy.

Small, steady changes in sleep, food and activity often make a bigger difference than strict diets.

Long-term health and PCOS

Women with PCOS have a higher risk of type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol, sleep apnoea and, if periods are very infrequent, thickening of the lining of the uterus. Regular check-ups of blood sugar, weight and blood pressure, and making sure you have a period at least every three months (naturally or with treatment), help protect your health.

Practical lifestyle tips

  • Choose whole grains, lentils, vegetables and protein; limit sugary drinks and sweets.
  • Aim for at least 30 minutes of walking or other activity on most days.
  • Get enough sleep — poor sleep worsens insulin resistance.
  • Set realistic, small goals; progress matters more than perfection.

Frequently asked questions

Can women with PCOS get pregnant?

Yes, many do — sometimes naturally and sometimes with help such as ovulation induction.

Is PCOS curable?

PCOS cannot be cured, but its symptoms can be controlled very well with the right treatment and lifestyle.

Do thin women get PCOS?

Yes. PCOS can affect women of any body weight.

Does PCOS affect unmarried girls?

Yes. It often starts in the teenage years with irregular periods or acne. Early care helps.

Will I need medicine for life?

Not necessarily. Treatment depends on your symptoms and goals, and many women manage with lifestyle changes and short courses of treatment.
