Infertility Evaluation

If you have been trying to conceive without success, a fertility evaluation looks for possible reasons in both partners. Understanding the cause is the first step towards the right treatment — and often the simplest one.

About Infertility Evaluation

Infertility is common. The World Health Organization estimates that around one in six people experience infertility at some point in their lives. It is not a reason for blame, and in many cases it can be helped.

A fertility evaluation is a planned set of tests for both the woman and the man. It aims to answer three questions: Is ovulation happening? Are the fallopian tubes and uterus healthy? Is the sperm healthy? Dr. Jawaria Rasheed guides couples in Bahawalpur through these tests in a clear, organised way, avoiding unnecessary investigations.

When to seek a fertility evaluation

  • ✓No pregnancy after 12 months of regular, unprotected intercourse
  • ✓After 6 months if the woman is 35 years or older
  • ✓Irregular or absent periods
  • ✓Known PCOS, endometriosis or fibroids
  • ✓Previous pelvic infection, surgery or ectopic pregnancy
  • ✓Two or more miscarriages
  • ✓Known male problems, such as previous testicular surgery

Common causes of infertility

  • Ovulation problems — for example PCOS, thyroid disorders or high prolactin.
  • Blocked or damaged fallopian tubes — often after infection or endometriosis.
  • Uterine factors — fibroids, polyps or scarring.
  • Male factors — low sperm count, poor movement or abnormal shape. Male factors play a part in a large share of cases.
  • Age — egg number and quality fall gradually, especially after 35.
  • Unexplained infertility — when tests are normal but pregnancy has not happened.

Tests that may be advised

  • Detailed history for both partners, including periods, previous pregnancies and lifestyle.
  • Hormone blood tests such as FSH, LH, AMH, thyroid and prolactin, timed to your cycle.
  • Pelvic ultrasound to check the uterus and ovaries.
  • Follicle tracking to confirm ovulation.
  • Semen analysis for the male partner.
  • Tubal test such as HSG (an X-ray dye test) to check that the tubes are open.

Treatment options after evaluation

Lifestyle and timing advice

Healthy weight, stopping smoking and timing intercourse with ovulation.

Treating the cause

Correcting thyroid or prolactin problems, or treating infections.

Ovulation induction

Medicines with ultrasound monitoring to help an egg grow and release.

IUI

Placing prepared sperm in the uterus at the time of ovulation.

Referral for IVF or surgery

When tubes are blocked, sperm counts are very low or other treatments have not worked.

Coming for your first fertility visit

  • Come together as a couple if possible.
  • Bring all previous reports, scans and semen analyses.
  • Note your cycle dates for the last few months.
  • Ask the clinic about the best cycle day for blood tests and scans.

Male fertility matters too

A semen analysis checks the number of sperm, how well they move and their shape. Results can vary from one sample to another, so an abnormal result is often repeated. Illness with fever, smoking, heat exposure, obesity and some medicines can affect sperm. Many male factors can be improved or bypassed with treatment such as IUI.

Age and fertility

A woman is born with all the eggs she will ever have. Their number and quality fall gradually with age, more quickly after 35. This is why women over 35 are advised to seek help after six months of trying, and why fertility tests such as AMH and an antral follicle count may be offered earlier.

The emotional side of infertility is real. Couples are encouraged to support each other and ask questions — there is no such thing as a silly question in a fertility consultation.

Frequently asked questions

Do both partners need to come?

It is very helpful. Male factors are common, and a semen test is simple, quick and important.

How long does the evaluation take?

Most tests are completed within one or two menstrual cycles, because some must be done on specific cycle days.

Is AMH alone enough to judge fertility?

No. AMH reflects egg reserve, not egg quality or the chance of natural pregnancy. It is interpreted together with age and other tests.

Is every couple offered IUI?

No. Treatment depends on the results. Some couples need only ovulation support or lifestyle changes; others are referred for IVF.

Is the HSG test painful?

Most women feel cramping similar to period pain for a few minutes. Taking a pain reliever beforehand usually helps.
