Period Problems

Periods that are very heavy, painful, irregular or missed are common — but they are not something you simply have to live with. Finding the cause allows effective treatment and protects your long-term health.

About Period Problems

A typical menstrual cycle lasts 21 to 35 days, with bleeding for up to 7 days. Changes outside this pattern can be caused by hormones, stress, thyroid problems, PCOS, fibroids, polyps, infections or, less commonly, more serious conditions.

Heavy bleeding over months can lead to anaemia, causing tiredness, breathlessness and poor concentration. Dr. Jawaria Rasheed assesses period problems carefully in Bahawalpur and offers treatment that fits your age, symptoms and family plans.

When to get checked

  • ✓Bleeding that soaks a pad every one to two hours, or passing large clots
  • ✓Periods lasting more than 7 days
  • ✓Pain that stops you from daily activities or school/work
  • ✓Cycles shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days
  • ✓Missed periods (when not pregnant)
  • ✓Bleeding between periods or after intercourse
  • ✓Any bleeding after menopause

Common causes

  • Hormonal imbalance — common in teenagers and around menopause.
  • PCOS — irregular or missed periods.
  • Thyroid disorders and high prolactin.
  • Fibroids and polyps — heavy or prolonged bleeding.
  • Endometriosis and adenomyosis — painful periods.
  • Infections and some contraceptive methods.

How the cause is found

  • A period diary — dates, how heavy each day is, and pain.
  • Examination when needed, with your consent.
  • Blood tests for anaemia, thyroid and hormones.
  • Pelvic ultrasound to look for fibroids, polyps or ovarian changes.
  • Further tests such as endometrial sampling in selected women.

Treatment options

Medicines for heavy bleeding

Non-hormonal tablets taken during periods to reduce blood loss.

Pain relief

Effective painkillers and hormonal options for painful periods.

Hormonal treatment

To regulate cycles and control bleeding.

Hormonal IUD (coil)

A long-acting option that greatly reduces bleeding for many women.

Treating the cause

Managing PCOS, thyroid problems, fibroids or polyps.

Iron replacement

To correct anaemia.

Keeping track

Keep a simple period diary on paper or a phone app. It helps your doctor see whether treatment is working. Eat iron-rich foods such as meat, lentils, eggs and leafy greens if you have heavy periods.

Painful periods (dysmenorrhoea)

Primary period pain starts in the teenage years and is caused by natural chemicals that make the uterus contract. It usually responds well to painkillers taken at the start of the period, heat and gentle exercise. Secondary period pain starts later or gets worse over time and may be due to endometriosis, adenomyosis, fibroids or infection — it should always be assessed.

Missed periods

A missed period should first prompt a pregnancy test. Other causes include stress, weight change, PCOS, thyroid problems, high prolactin, excessive exercise and approaching menopause. If you have missed three or more periods in a row, or periods have stopped before age 45, see a gynecologist.

Heavy bleeding and anaemia

Losing a lot of blood each month can lower your haemoglobin. Signs of anaemia include tiredness, pale skin, breathlessness on stairs, hair fall and poor concentration. A simple blood test confirms it, and iron treatment together with controlling the bleeding usually brings levels back to normal.

When to seek urgent care: Seek urgent care if bleeding is extremely heavy, or you feel dizzy, faint or breathless.

Frequently asked questions

Should I track my periods before the visit?

Yes. Note the start and end dates and how heavy each day is — a phone app or notebook is fine.

Are irregular periods normal in teenagers?

Cycles can be irregular in the first year or two after periods start, but very heavy, painful or absent periods should be checked.

Can stress affect my periods?

Yes. Stress, sudden weight change and intense exercise can delay or stop periods.

Is period pain always normal?

Mild cramps are common, but pain that stops daily life may be a sign of endometriosis or another condition.

Will hormonal treatment affect my fertility?

Most hormonal treatments do not affect long-term fertility, and cycles usually return after stopping.
