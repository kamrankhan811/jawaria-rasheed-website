Pregnancy & Antenatal Care

Antenatal care means regular check-ups during pregnancy to look after the health of both mother and baby. Good antenatal care finds problems early, keeps you informed at every stage and prepares you for a safe delivery.

About Pregnancy & Antenatal Care

Every pregnancy is different. Some mothers have a smooth journey, while others need closer monitoring because of high blood pressure, diabetes, anaemia, thyroid problems or difficulties in a previous pregnancy. Regular visits with an experienced gynecologist mean these issues are picked up early and managed safely.

Dr. Jawaria Rasheed provides antenatal care in Bahawalpur from the first positive test through to planning your delivery, with clear advice on diet, supplements, activity and the warning signs every mother should know.

When to book your first pregnancy visit

  • ✓You have a positive pregnancy test (ideally before 8–10 weeks)
  • ✓You are planning a pregnancy and want advice beforehand
  • ✓You had a miscarriage, premature birth or caesarean section before
  • ✓You have diabetes, high blood pressure, thyroid disease or epilepsy
  • ✓You are expecting twins, or are under 18 or over 35
  • ✓You conceived after fertility treatment such as IUI

What antenatal check-ups include

  • Confirming the pregnancy and calculating your due date.
  • Blood tests for blood group, haemoglobin, blood sugar, thyroid and infections, as advised.
  • Ultrasound scans at the right stages to check the baby’s growth and position.
  • Routine checks of blood pressure, weight and urine at each visit.
  • Advice on nutrition, supplements, rest, exercise and travel.

Care through each trimester

First trimester (up to 12 weeks)

Dating scan, booking tests, folic acid, and help with nausea and tiredness.

Second trimester (13–27 weeks)

Anomaly scan, iron and calcium review, blood sugar testing when advised.

Third trimester (28 weeks onwards)

Growth checks, baby’s movements, blood pressure monitoring and delivery planning.

High-risk pregnancy

Closer follow-up for diabetes, high blood pressure, twins or previous complications.

Visit schedule

Most mothers are seen about once a month until 28 weeks, every two weeks until 36 weeks and then weekly until delivery. Your schedule may be adjusted to your health and your baby’s needs.

Bring your antenatal file, reports and scans to every visit, and note any questions in advance.

Healthy habits during pregnancy

  • Eat a balanced diet with protein, fruit, vegetables, dairy and whole grains.
  • Drink plenty of water and rest when you feel tired.
  • Take only the medicines prescribed for you — check before taking anything new, including herbal remedies.
  • Stay active with gentle walking unless advised otherwise.
  • Avoid smoking and second-hand smoke.
  • Keep your antenatal file with you when you travel.

Common conditions that need extra care

Anaemia is very common in pregnancy and is treated with iron. Gestational diabetes is high blood sugar that develops during pregnancy; it is found by a blood sugar test and controlled with diet and sometimes medicine. High blood pressure and pre-eclampsia need close monitoring because they can affect both mother and baby. Regular check-ups are the best way to pick these up early.

When to seek urgent care: Go to the nearest hospital emergency department straight away if you have vaginal bleeding, severe abdominal pain, fits, a severe headache with blurred vision, swelling of the face or hands, leaking fluid, fever, or if your baby’s movements reduce or stop.

Frequently asked questions

When should I have my first antenatal visit?

Ideally soon after a positive pregnancy test, usually between 6 and 10 weeks.

Which supplements should I take?

Folic acid is advised before conception and in the first three months. Iron, calcium and vitamin D are often needed later; your doctor will advise based on your blood tests.

How many ultrasound scans are needed?

Most pregnancies need a few key scans — early dating, an anomaly scan around 18–22 weeks and growth scans later if required. Extra scans are done only when there is a medical reason.

Is it safe to exercise during pregnancy?

For most healthy pregnancies, gentle activity such as walking is beneficial. Ask your doctor if you have any complications.

Will I need a caesarean section?

Many women can deliver normally. A caesarean may be advised for specific medical reasons, and your doctor will discuss the safest option for you and your baby.
