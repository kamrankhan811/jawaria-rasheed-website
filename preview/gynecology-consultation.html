About Gynecology Consultation

Many women put off seeing a gynecologist because of hesitation, embarrassment or a busy family schedule. Yet a single consultation can often explain symptoms that have been worrying you for months — and early advice usually means simpler, quicker treatment.

A consultation is not only for illness. It is also the right place to ask about planning a pregnancy, choosing contraception, understanding test results or simply having a routine women’s health check-up. Every visit with Dr. Jawaria is private and respectful, and you are welcome to bring your mother, sister or husband with you.

When should you see a gynecologist?

  • ✓Periods that are irregular, very painful, very heavy or have stopped
  • ✓Bleeding between periods, after intercourse or after menopause
  • ✓Unusual vaginal discharge, itching, burning or odour
  • ✓Lower abdominal or pelvic pain
  • ✓Pain during intercourse
  • ✓Difficulty getting pregnant, or planning a pregnancy
  • ✓Questions about contraception, hormones or menopause
  • ✓A routine women’s health check-up or screening

What happens during the consultation

  • Your story: Dr. Jawaria asks about your symptoms, periods, pregnancies and general health.
  • Examination: only if it is needed, explained beforehand and done with your permission.
  • Tests: an ultrasound, blood tests or a urine test may be advised to confirm the cause.
  • Explanation: you are told what the findings mean, in plain language.
  • Plan: treatment options are discussed so you can decide together.

How to prepare for your visit

  • Note the first day of your last period and how regular your cycles are.
  • Bring previous reports, ultrasound scans and prescriptions.
  • Make a list of medicines you take, including supplements.
  • Write down your questions so you don’t forget them.

You can usually be seen during your period for most problems. If a specific test such as a Pap smear is planned, the clinic will tell you the best day to come.

Gynecology care at every age

Teenage years: period problems, painful periods, acne and early signs of PCOS are common reasons for a first visit. Consultations for young girls are gentle, and an internal examination is usually not needed.

Reproductive years: infections, contraception, pre-pregnancy advice, fertility concerns and screening tests.

Around and after menopause: irregular bleeding, hot flushes, bone health and any bleeding after menopause.

Common tests explained

Pelvic ultrasound uses sound waves to look at the uterus and ovaries. It is painless and does not use radiation. Blood tests check hormones, blood count, thyroid and blood sugar. A Pap smear screens the cervix for early changes, and a vaginal swab can identify the exact cause of an infection so the right treatment is given the first time.

Tests are advised only when they will change the treatment plan, so you avoid unnecessary cost and worry.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to prepare for my first visit?

No special preparation is needed. Note the date of your last period and bring any previous reports. Wear comfortable clothing.

Will I definitely be examined?

No. An examination is done only if it will help, and only after it has been explained and you agree. You can ask for a family member or female staff member to be present.

Can I come during my period?

Yes, for most consultations. Bleeding problems are often easier to assess when they are happening.

Can unmarried girls see a gynecologist?

Yes. Period problems, PCOS and other concerns are common in teenagers and young women. Examinations are adapted to the patient and are only done if needed.

How long does a consultation take?

Usually 15–30 minutes, depending on your concern and whether a scan is needed.
