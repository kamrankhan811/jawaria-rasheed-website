Menopause Care

Menopause is a natural stage of life when periods stop permanently, usually between the ages of 45 and 55. The years around it — perimenopause — can bring hot flushes, night sweats, poor sleep, mood changes and vaginal dryness. Support and treatment are available.

About Menopause Care

Menopause is confirmed when a woman has had no periods for 12 months. Falling oestrogen levels cause many of the symptoms and also affect bone strength and heart health over time.

Many women silently cope with symptoms that could be eased. Dr. Jawaria Rasheed in Bahawalpur offers practical menopause care, from lifestyle advice to medical treatment, tailored to each woman’s health history.

Common symptoms

  • ✓Irregular periods in your 40s
  • ✓Hot flushes and night sweats
  • ✓Poor sleep and tiredness
  • ✓Mood changes, anxiety or low mood
  • ✓Vaginal dryness and discomfort during intercourse
  • ✓Frequent urination or urinary infections
  • ✓Joint aches and reduced concentration

What the consultation covers

  • Your symptoms and how they affect daily life.
  • Health risks — blood pressure, weight, family history.
  • Tests where needed, such as blood sugar, cholesterol and thyroid.
  • Screening — Pap smear and breast checks as advised.

Treatment options

Lifestyle

Regular exercise, a calcium-rich diet, healthy weight and limiting caffeine.

Hormone therapy (HRT)

Very effective for hot flushes in suitable women, after a careful risk review.

Non-hormonal medicines

For women who cannot or prefer not to take hormones.

Vaginal treatments

Moisturisers and low-dose local therapy for dryness.

Bone health

Calcium, vitamin D and bone density testing when indicated.

Staying healthy after menopause

Annual check-ups help catch high blood pressure, diabetes and bone thinning early. Stay active, keep a healthy weight and continue recommended screening.

Perimenopause: the years before menopause

Symptoms often start in the early to mid-40s, while periods are still happening but becoming irregular. Periods may come closer together, further apart or become heavier. This is a good time to talk to a gynecologist about symptoms, contraception and long-term health.

Bone and heart health

After menopause, bone loss speeds up, increasing the risk of osteoporosis and fractures. Weight-bearing exercise such as walking, enough calcium and vitamin D, not smoking and, in some women, hormone therapy help protect bones. Heart disease risk also rises, so checking blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol becomes more important.

Emotional wellbeing

Mood swings, irritability, low mood and anxiety are common during the menopause transition, often made worse by poor sleep. Talking about these changes with your family and your doctor is important — effective help is available.

When to seek urgent care: Any vaginal bleeding after menopause must be checked promptly.

Frequently asked questions

Is hormone therapy safe?

It is suitable for many women, but not for everyone. Your doctor will look at your personal health history before advising.

How long do hot flushes last?

Often several years, but it varies widely between women.

Can I still get pregnant around menopause?

Yes, until 12 months without a period (or 2 years if under 50). Contraception is still needed until then.

What is early menopause?

Menopause before 45. It should be assessed, as treatment helps protect bones and heart health.

Does diet help menopause symptoms?

A balanced, calcium-rich diet and regular activity support bone health and overall wellbeing.
