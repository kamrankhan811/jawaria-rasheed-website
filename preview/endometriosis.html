Endometriosis & Pelvic Pain

Endometriosis is a condition in which tissue similar to the lining of the uterus grows outside it — on the ovaries, tubes or other pelvic organs. It can cause painful periods, pain during intercourse, long-term pelvic pain and difficulty conceiving.

About Endometriosis & Pelvic Pain

Endometriosis affects around one in ten women of reproductive age worldwide, yet it often takes years to diagnose because severe period pain is wrongly considered “normal”. The tissue responds to monthly hormones, causing inflammation, scarring and sometimes ovarian cysts (endometriomas, or “chocolate cysts”).

Not all pelvic pain is endometriosis — infections, adenomyosis, cysts, bowel and bladder problems can also cause pain. Dr. Jawaria Rasheed in Bahawalpur takes pelvic pain seriously and works step by step to find the cause and relieve it.

Symptoms to look out for

  • ✓Period pain that is severe or getting worse over time
  • ✓Pelvic pain between periods
  • ✓Pain during or after intercourse
  • ✓Painful bowel movements or urination during periods
  • ✓Heavy or irregular bleeding
  • ✓Tiredness
  • ✓Difficulty getting pregnant

How endometriosis is assessed

  • Detailed history of your pain and its pattern.
  • Examination, with your consent.
  • Ultrasound, which can detect endometriomas and some deep disease.
  • Laparoscopy (keyhole surgery) in selected women, to confirm and treat the condition.

Treatment options

Pain relief

Regular painkillers, started before the period when advised.

Hormonal treatment

Pills, progestogens or a hormonal IUD to calm the disease and reduce pain.

Laparoscopic surgery

To remove endometriosis or cysts, usually by referral to a specialist surgical team.

Fertility treatment

Ovulation induction, IUI or IVF depending on severity.

Supportive care

Exercise, heat therapy and help with sleep and stress.

Long-term care

Endometriosis can return after treatment, so follow-up is important. A pain diary helps you and your doctor see which treatments work best.

Why diagnosis is often delayed

Many women are told that painful periods are normal, or their symptoms are mistaken for stomach or bladder problems. Pain that prevents school, work or daily activities, or that keeps getting worse, deserves a proper assessment. Keeping a record of when the pain happens and how severe it is helps your doctor.

Endometriosis and fertility

Endometriosis can affect fertility by causing inflammation, scarring around the tubes and ovaries, and ovarian cysts. Mild disease may only slightly reduce fertility, and many women conceive naturally or with ovulation induction and IUI. More severe disease may need surgery or IVF. Any surgery on the ovaries should be planned carefully to protect egg reserve.

Living with endometriosis

Regular exercise, a heat pad during flares, good sleep and a balanced diet can help alongside medical treatment. Endometriosis is a long-term condition, but with the right plan most women gain good control of their symptoms.

Frequently asked questions

Does endometriosis always cause infertility?

No. Many women with endometriosis conceive. Some need fertility treatment, which your doctor can discuss.

Can endometriosis be seen on ultrasound?

Ovarian cysts and some deep endometriosis can be seen, but small patches may not show. A normal scan does not completely rule it out.

Will pregnancy cure endometriosis?

Symptoms often improve during pregnancy, but they can return afterwards. Pregnancy is not a treatment.

Is surgery always needed?

No. Many women manage well with medicines.

What is adenomyosis?

A related condition in which the lining grows into the muscle of the uterus, causing heavy, painful periods.
