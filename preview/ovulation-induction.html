Ovulation Induction & Follicle Monitoring

Ovulation induction uses medicines to help the ovaries grow and release an egg. Follicle monitoring (follicular tracking) uses ultrasound scans to follow the egg’s growth, so that intercourse or IUI can be timed at the best moment.

About Ovulation Induction & Follicle Monitoring

Every month, a healthy ovary develops a fluid-filled sac called a follicle, which contains an egg. When it is mature, the follicle releases the egg — this is ovulation. If ovulation is irregular or absent, pregnancy is difficult.

Ovulation induction is one of the most common and effective first-line fertility treatments, especially for women with PCOS. Monitoring makes it safer: it shows when the egg is ready and helps avoid an over-response of the ovaries or multiple pregnancy.

Who may need ovulation induction?

  • ✓Irregular, infrequent or absent periods
  • ✓PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome)
  • ✓Ovulation not confirmed on tests
  • ✓Unexplained infertility
  • ✓As part of an IUI cycle

Before treatment starts

  • Baseline ultrasound early in the cycle to check the ovaries and lining.
  • Hormone tests including thyroid and prolactin.
  • Semen analysis for the husband.
  • Tubal assessment when indicated.

How a monitored cycle works

Medicines

Tablets (such as letrozole or clomiphene) or injections, started early in the cycle as prescribed.

Follicle scans

Usually 2–4 ultrasound scans a few days apart to measure follicle size and the lining.

Trigger injection

Given when a follicle reaches the right size, to time ovulation.

Timed intercourse or IUI

Your doctor advises the best days to try, or schedules IUI.

Progesterone support

Sometimes advised after ovulation.

Side effects and safety

Most women tolerate treatment well. Some notice hot flushes, mood changes, bloating or mild pelvic discomfort. Careful dose adjustment and scans reduce the chance of producing too many eggs, which can lead to multiple pregnancy or ovarian hyperstimulation.

If too many follicles grow, your doctor may advise cancelling or adjusting that cycle for your safety.

Understanding your scan results

On each scan, the doctor measures the size of the growing follicles in millimetres and the thickness of the uterine lining. A follicle usually releases its egg when it reaches about 18–22 mm. The lining should become thicker and have a healthy pattern before ovulation. These measurements help decide the timing of the trigger injection, intercourse or IUI.

Letrozole and clomiphene

Both are tablets commonly used to encourage ovulation. Letrozole is often preferred for women with PCOS. They are usually taken for five days early in the cycle. Injections (gonadotrophins) are used when tablets do not work or for some IUI cycles. Never take these medicines without supervision, as the dose must be adjusted to your response.

Frequently asked questions

How many scans will I need?

Usually two to four scans in a cycle, a few days apart, depending on how the follicles grow.

How many cycles of ovulation induction can I try?

Typically several cycles are tried before changing the plan. Your doctor will review results after each cycle.

Will I have twins?

The chance of twins is higher than in natural conception, which is why monitoring is important.

Can I take ovulation tablets without monitoring?

It is not recommended. Without scans, you cannot know whether you ovulated or whether too many follicles grew.

What should I do on scan days?

Come at the time given by the clinic. A full bladder is usually not needed for vaginal scans.
