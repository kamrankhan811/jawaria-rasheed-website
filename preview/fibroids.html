Uterine Fibroids

Fibroids are non-cancerous growths of the muscle of the uterus. Many cause no symptoms at all; others cause heavy periods, pain, pressure or fertility problems. Treatment depends on your symptoms, not just on the size of the fibroid.

About Uterine Fibroids

Fibroids are very common, especially in women in their 30s and 40s. They range from the size of a pea to larger than a melon, and a woman may have one or several. Their position matters: fibroids inside the cavity of the uterus are more likely to cause heavy bleeding and affect fertility than those on the outside.

Fibroids almost never become cancerous. Dr. Jawaria Rasheed in Bahawalpur helps women understand their scan results and choose between monitoring, medicines and surgery.

Symptoms of fibroids

  • ✓Heavy or prolonged periods, sometimes with clots
  • ✓Anaemia, tiredness and breathlessness
  • ✓Pelvic pressure, bloating or a swollen lower abdomen
  • ✓Frequent urination or constipation
  • ✓Lower back or pelvic pain
  • ✓Difficulty conceiving or repeated miscarriage

How fibroids are assessed

  • Pelvic ultrasound to map the number, size and position of fibroids.
  • Blood tests for anaemia.
  • Further imaging such as MRI when surgery is being planned.

Treatment options

Watchful waiting

Small fibroids without symptoms are often simply monitored.

Medicines

To reduce heavy bleeding and pain, or to shrink fibroids before surgery.

Hormonal IUD

Can reduce bleeding in suitable women.

Myomectomy

Surgery to remove fibroids while keeping the uterus — often advised for women who want children.

Hysterectomy

Removal of the uterus, considered only when other options are not suitable and childbearing is complete.

Fibroids and pregnancy

Many women with fibroids have healthy pregnancies. Depending on their position, fibroids may need closer monitoring during pregnancy. If you are planning a pregnancy, discuss your fibroids with your gynecologist beforehand.

Types of fibroids

Submucosal fibroids grow into the cavity of the uterus and most often cause heavy bleeding and fertility problems. Intramural fibroids grow within the muscle wall and can enlarge the uterus. Subserosal fibroids grow on the outer surface and may press on the bladder or bowel. Some fibroids grow on a stalk (pedunculated).

Who gets fibroids?

Fibroids are more common as women get older (until menopause), in women with a family history of fibroids, and in women who are overweight. Pregnancy and childbirth are associated with a lower risk. The exact cause is not fully known, but fibroid growth depends on the hormones oestrogen and progesterone.

Recovery after fibroid surgery

Recovery depends on the type of operation. Keyhole and hysteroscopic procedures usually allow a return to normal activities within days to a couple of weeks, while open surgery needs a longer recovery of several weeks. Your surgeon will advise on rest, lifting, and when it is safe to try for pregnancy.

Frequently asked questions

Do all fibroids need surgery?

No. Fibroids that cause no problems are usually monitored.

Can fibroids turn into cancer?

This is extremely rare. Fibroids are benign growths.

Do fibroids come back after removal?

New fibroids can grow after myomectomy, especially in younger women.

Do fibroids shrink after menopause?

Usually yes, because they depend on hormones that fall after menopause.

Can I get pregnant with fibroids?

Many women do. Some fibroids, especially inside the cavity, may need treatment first.
